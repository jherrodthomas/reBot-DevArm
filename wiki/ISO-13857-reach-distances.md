> **In one sentence.** ISO 13857 gives how far a hazard must be from a fence, an edge or an opening so that a hand, an arm or a foot cannot reach it, over, around or through.

## What it is

ISO 13857:2019, *Safety of machinery: safety distances to prevent hazard zones being reached by upper and lower
limbs*. A type-B standard of tables: reaching upwards, over a protective structure, around an edge, and through
openings of a given shape and size, for low and high risk.

## The idea in the picture

- **Over a structure.** Given the height of the hazard (a) and of the structure (b), the table gives the horizontal
  distance (c) the hazard must keep from the structure.
- **Through an opening.** Given the shape and size of the opening (e), the table gives the safety distance (sr)
  behind it: a slot a fingertip fits through needs only a short distance; an opening an arm fits through needs a long
  one.

## Why it matters for a desktop arm

On the supervised bench, nobody but the operator is meant to be near the arm, and the boundary is a marked area, not a
guard. If the arm is ever used where others pass, a classroom for example, a fence or enclosure would be sized with
this standard, and so would any guard over a joint.

## What the project does not claim

Any guard or distance. None is designed; the record's position is that nobody is inside the reach envelope while the
arm can move (GAP-06).

## Words you will meet

- **Hazard zone**: the space where a person can be harmed.
- **Protective structure**: a fence, guard or edge between a person and a hazard zone.
- **Safety distance (sr)**: the distance a protective structure or opening must keep from the hazard zone.
