> **In one sentence.** The AI Act is the EU law on artificial intelligence; for a machine, the question is whether an AI component is a safety component of a product that needs third-party assessment, because that makes it a high-risk AI system.

## What it is

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence. It entered into force on
1 August 2024 and applies in stages; which dates bind a high-risk system of this kind is **to be checked** against the
current text. Annex I lists Union product laws, the Machinery Regulation among them.

## The idea in the picture

Under Article 6(1), an AI system is **high-risk** when it is a safety component of a product, or is itself a
product, covered by a law listed in Annex I, and that product must undergo third-party conformity assessment. A
high-risk AI system carries duties for risk management, data and data governance, technical documentation, record
keeping, transparency, human oversight, and accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity, with a conformity assessment.

## Why it matters for a desktop arm

The arm runs learned policies. If one were made a safety component, the Machinery Regulation would call for a notified
body (its Annex I Part A), and the AI Act would make the policy a high-risk AI system on top. Keeping every learned
component outside the safety functions keeps both thresholds uncrossed, and the record monitors that threshold.

## What the project does not claim

That the arm is outside the Act. Whether the policies are a safety component depends on an open decision, and other
duties of the Act may apply whatever the answer.

## Words you will meet

- **AI system**: the Act's defined term for a machine-based system that infers from its input how to generate
  outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.
- **Safety component**: a component that fulfils a safety function, or whose failure endangers health and safety.
- **High-risk AI system**: one the Act classifies as high-risk, under Article 6.
